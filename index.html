Colorado Springs, CO · Data center transparency

You can't make a good decision about a data center if nobody can check its numbers.

OpenGrid Springs gives Colorado Springs residents accurate, verified information about local data centers' energy and water use, so the community can make informed decisions when new data centers are proposed.

The plan in four steps

  1. 1

    Report

    Every quarter, data centers publish their power and water use.

  2. 2

    Audit

    An independent third party checks the numbers against utility meter data.

  3. 3

    Publish

    Verified numbers go on a public city dashboard anyone can read.

  4. 4

    Enforce

    Fines for late, missing or inaccurate reports keep the numbers honest.

Why it matters

Both sides have a point. Neither side has checked numbers.

What data centers offer

  • 40–100 permanent jobs claimed for Project Taurus.
  • Raeden says more than $3M a year in tax revenue.
  • Construction jobs and grid upgrades.
  • The internet, cloud and AI services everyone uses.
  • Colorado Springs Utilities has a dedicated large-load rate for 10 MW+ customers, designed so they cover their own costs.

What residents worry about

  • Electricity demand: a 50 MW center would be the utility's largest customer.
  • Water use in a drought-prone region.
  • Higher utility bills.
  • Noise, heat and backup generators.
  • Claims that nobody independently verifies.

Every claim on both sides is either a company promise or a resident fear. Our ordinance turns both into checked numbers.

The power-versus-water tradeoff

Cooling is where power and water trade off. Saving one usually costs the other.

Evaporative (water) cooling

PowerLower
WaterHigher

Spraying or evaporating water carries heat away cheaply, so the building uses less electricity, but it consumes water.

⇄

Air or closed-loop cooling

PowerHigher
WaterLower

Fans, chillers and sealed loops save water, but they need more electricity to move the heat.

A good PUE can hide a bad WUE, so we need both.

Plain-English glossary

PUE · Power Usage Effectiveness

Total facility energy divided by the energy used by the computers. 1.0 is perfect; 1.3 means 30% overhead for cooling, lighting and other systems.

WUE · Water Usage Effectiveness

Liters of water used per kilowatt-hour (kWh) of computer energy. Lower is better.

MW · Megawatt

A measure of power at a moment in time. 1 MW powers about 600–1,000 homes.

MWh · Megawatt-hour

A measure of energy over time: 1 MW running for one hour. Your power bill counts kWh; 1 MWh is 1,000 kWh.

Acre-foot

The water needed to cover one acre one foot deep: about 326,000 gallons. Western water rights are often counted this way.

Third-party audit

A check by an independent, certified reviewer who is not paid by the company to agree with it. They compare reported numbers to meter data and records.

Proposed Colorado Springs ordinance

The Data Center Resource Act

Working name. Report quarterly, audit yearly, publish everything that matters, and fine what's late or wrong.

Who's covered

Data centers drawing 1 MW or more.

  • Small 1–10 MW
  • Large 10 MW+, matching Colorado Springs Utilities' large-load rate line

Reported every quarter

  • Total electricity (MWh) and renewable share
  • Peak demand (MW)
  • Total water (gallons) and source: city, well or reclaimed
  • PUE and WUE
  • Cooling method

Audits

An independent, city-certified auditor checks each facility once a year against utility meter data.

Operators pay an annual audit fee, and all fines go into the audit fund. Reports more than 10% off the audited numbers count as inaccurate.

Public

A city-run dashboard, updated quarterly. This site is the prototype.

Trade secrets like customer names stay private. Total power and water use never do.

Why it's fair

A 50 MW center uses about 438,000 MWh a year. At an assumed ~$70/MWh, that's roughly $30M in electricity (estimate).

The $100,000 cap is about 0.3% of that bill. Reporting is cheaper than not reporting.

Thin sliver = max yearly late-report fine vs. a year of power.
